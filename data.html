Tracking Hawai‘i’s Shoreline Change & Coastal Erosion

Explore historical shoreline data, analyze erosion patterns, and visualize long-term coastal change across Hawai‘i’s islands using transects and shoreline measurements.

Project Overview

This project investigates coastal erosion across Hawai‘i using historical shoreline data from Oʻahu, Maui, and Kauaʻi. By analyzing shoreline positions from aerial imagery and coastal surveys, long-term trends in beach erosion and shoreline change can be identified.

Data Preparation

Historical shoreline positions were obtained from aerial imagery identifying the Low Water Mark (LWM). Datasets range from the early 1900s to 2018 and include positional uncertainty estimates between roughly 10 and 40 feet depending on image quality and year.

Methodology

Shoreline change was measured using transect analysis. Transects placed perpendicular to the coastline compare shoreline positions from different years to calculate shoreline change rates in feet per year (ft/yr).

Analysis

Results show major regional differences in shoreline change. Waikīkī, Māʻili, and ʻEwa Beach on Oʻahu experience some of the highest erosion rates, while locations such as Kahuku Beach show shoreline growth due to sediment accumulation.

Key Findings

Oʻahu shows the fastest erosion rates, often exceeding –3 ft per year in several locations. Maui and Kauaʻi generally show moderate erosion, though rates vary depending on coastal conditions and sediment transport.

Data Highlights

Fastest erosion observed at Waikīkī Beach (up to –4.6 ft/yr). High erosion also occurs at Māʻili and ʻEwa Beach (~ –3 to –3.9 ft/yr), while Kahuku Beach shows shoreline growth between +1.8 and +2.6 ft/yr. Historical shoreline records span from 1912 to 2018.
